Chapter one

Work is a solo story.
It shouldn't be.

You talk to yourself at your desk. Everyone who works alone does. You read an email out loud in disbelief. You ask the empty room where that file went. You say "okay" to nobody before the hard part. That's not a quirk. That's a person built for company, doing a two-person job alone.

Every story we love already knows this. The hero flies, but somebody is on the wing. Nobody keeps watch alone.

You're the hero of your story. Sidekick is the one beside you.

Chapter two

No window. No box.
Just say it.

There's nothing to open and nowhere to type. Sidekick sits in your menu bar and listens while she's awake, so help starts the way it should — you say something out loud, mid-thought, hands still on the keys.

Move my two o'clock to Thursday and invite Sam.

Done. Thursday at two, Sam's on the invite.

Text Mom that I'll call after dinner.

Here's the text: "I'll call you after dinner." Send it?

Where did I put the Henderson contract?

It's in Downloads. Want it in the Henderson folder instead?

Her replies are short on purpose. Good company doesn't lecture. She says the useful thing and lets you get back to it.

And the icon in your menu bar always tells you the truth about what she's doing — including on this page. It's at the top right now.

Chapter three

A sidekick with
actual hands.

Most assistants talk. This one does.

Calendar, guests included. Reminders set, notes taken.

Email — drafted, and sent. Texts sent too, and she reads every one back to you before it goes.

People found in your Contacts. Documents found on your disk, and filed where they belong.

Typing while you dictate. Keys pressed, buttons clicked, menus worked, in your real apps.

Windows read as text and explained. Browser tabs listed, switched, closed.

Shortcuts run. Music controlled. Volume, brightness, Do Not Disturb, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth.

She only ever works inside your home folder, and anything she puts in the trash can come back out.

If you'd do it with your own two hands at this Mac, odds are you can just ask. A sidekick who can't carry anything is just a narrator.

Chapter four

She keeps watch,
so you don't have to.

Ask, and she looks at your screen — with a visible flash and a shutter sound, so a look is never a secret.

Ask, and she watches. A single window or the whole screen, with a soft glow along the edge the entire time she's watching — like the one around this page right now. Snapshots, not video.

And here is the part that feels like having someone in the room: tell her what you're waiting for, and she speaks up when it appears. The build finishing. The reply landing. The name you've been refreshing for. You stop checking. She keeps watch.

No flash, no glow, no looking. That's a promise built into the product, not a line in a policy.

A tool waits to be used. Company notices.

Chapter five

She was there yesterday.

Ask an assistant about Tuesday and you get nothing. Ask someone who was in the room and you get an answer.

Sidekick remembers your days together. What you looked at and what you talked about is distilled into memory that lives in one folder on your Mac, and nowhere else. Not on a server. Not in an account, because there is no account. Open the folder and read every word she knows. Drag it to the trash and she starts fresh.

Your history together is yours — literally a folder you can hold.

Chapter six

Loyal, in the old sense
of the word.

Someone who is always listening has to earn it.

So Sidekick cannot run terminal commands and cannot install software. Not won't — can't. We left those hands off on purpose, so leaving her awake is a comfortable thing to do.

Her menu-bar icon never lies. Eight states, one for everything she could be doing, and the icon you see is the state she is in. Listening looks like listening. Watching glows. Asleep looks asleep.

And when a macOS permission is off, she doesn't shrug and fail quietly. She names the exact switch to flip.

Loyalty used to mean something simple: keeps your secrets, tells you the truth, stays.

Questions

Asked, answered.

Is she really always listening?

While she's awake, yes — that's what makes her company instead of an app you have to open. After a few quiet minutes she puts herself to sleep, and asleep she hears nothing and costs nothing. The menu-bar icon always shows her true state, so you never have to wonder.

Can she see my screen without me knowing?

No. Every look comes with a visible flash and a shutter sound. When she's watching a window or your screen over time, a glow sits along the edge the entire time — and she's taking snapshots, not recording video. No flash, no glow, no looking.

Where does what she remembers live?

In one folder on your Mac, and nowhere else. Nothing is synced, and there's no account to sync to. Open the folder and read everything she knows, or drag it to the trash and start over.

What can't she do?

She cannot run terminal commands and cannot install software — we built her without those hands on purpose, so she's safe to leave listening. She only works with files in your home folder, anything she trashes is recoverable, and she reads every text back to you before it sends.

What do I need to run her?

A Mac with Apple Silicon. The download is signed and notarized by Apple, so it opens without warnings. The free plan needs no account and no key — or bring your own free Gemini key for unlimited awake time. When she needs a macOS permission, she tells you the exact switch to turn on.
