Where it sits

A fourth kind of AI.

The others wait in a tab, drive your computer for you, or film everything just in case. Sidekick does none of those.

Not a chatbot

You don't go to it and describe your problem. It's already looking at the thing you're asking about, so the context is the screen itself — and the answer comes back as a voice, not a wall of text you have to read.

Not an agent

It never takes the wheel. You stay the one working; it helps at the speed of talking. Every action it takes happens visibly, at your request, and stops the moment you say so.

Not a recorder

It isn't archiving your life in the background. It looks when you ask, with a flash you can see and a colored pill in your menu bar — and what it keeps never leaves your machine.

Always honest

You can always tell what it's doing.

One glance at the menu bar. No hidden states, no ambiguity about whether something is listening or looking.

Privacy

Your screen never leaves your Mac.

Every screenshot lands in one folder — ~/Library/Application Support/Sidekick/memory — next to a small database of what was said and learned. Nothing is uploaded, synced, or backed up. There's no account and no cloud library, so there's no copy of your life for anyone to ask us for.

Only when you ask

Captures happen at your request or while you've told it to watch. Never in the background, always with a flash, a glow, or a colored pill.

What goes out

The moment you ask, that one image and your voice go to Google's Gemini to be understood — like any assistant. Nothing else, and nothing kept for us.

What we can see

On the free plan our server stores four things: a random install ID, a date, a token count, a timestamp. No screenshots. No transcripts. No name or email.

Yours to erase

A switch stops memory entirely. A button wipes every screenshot and transcript. Or drag that folder to the trash — that really is all of it.

Questions

The things people ask.

Is it listening all the time?

While it's awake and unmuted, yes — that's what makes it a sidekick instead of an app you open. The menu-bar pill never lies about it: green means listening, red means muted, a moon means asleep. Asleep is a real disconnect that costs nothing.

Can it see my screen without asking?

No. It has no capture running at rest. When it looks, you get a white flash and a shutter; when it watches continuously, your screen edge glows the whole time.

What can it actually do to my Mac?

Open sites, apps and files, find documents, read and write your clipboard, type and paste for you, put things on your calendar, add reminders, write notes, draft mail for you to send, and control music. It can't run terminal commands or install anything — deliberately.

Why does macOS warn me when I install?

This early build isn't notarized with Apple yet, so the first launch needs a right-click → Open. Signed builds are coming.

Windows?

It's built cross-platform and a Windows version is on the way.
